Facility design & buildout

Concept to Canopy.

Cultivation, post-harvest, and packaging designed as one system — and, if you want it, built through a partner network with one point of contact from empty building to first harvest.

Designed by someone who has to run it afterward.

Most facilities are designed by people who will never harvest a room in them. The rooms are pretty, the mechanicals are undersized at week six, the dry room is an afterthought, and the workflow makes every tech walk twice as far as they should.

We design the way we operate: canopy first, then environment, then the flow of plants and people through the building — from propagation through veg, flower, dry, cure, trim, and package. Every room gets sized against a real production model, not a square-footage guess.

Room layoutsCanopy math, aisle logic, and plant flow across every stage
HVAC & mechanicalSensible and latent load sizing for the plants you'll actually grow
LightingFixture selection, PPFD targets, and spectrum by stage
AirflowUnder-canopy and overhead circulation designed as a system
BenchingRolling, tiered, or fixed — chosen for labor, not looks
FertigationInjection, recipes, zoning, and runoff handling
ControlsRoom controllers, sensors, and the dashboards to run it by the numbers
Post-harvest & packagingDry, cure, trim, and production sized to the flower coming out of the rooms

The Menu

The bar isn't average.
It's the best.

What makes great cannabis great — and the standard every room we touch is held to. This is the why behind everything else on this site.

Plant first. Always.

Every decision in a grow is a decision about the plant. The environment, the feed, the schedule, the team, the building — they exist to give a plant what it needs to express its genetics fully. When the plant is the customer, quality stops being a marketing word.

We think about flower the way a great kitchen thinks about a plate: the ingredients, the technique, the timing, and the consistency all have to be right, every time, or it isn't the dish. Michelin-grade craft isn't a slogan. It's a way of holding ourselves to the top of the category instead of the middle.

Six disciplines

Craft is the sum of six things done well.

I

Genetics

You cannot grow past the ceiling of the cultivar. Hunt for potency, terpenes, structure, and yield together — then keep the winners clean.

II

Environment

Light, temperature, humidity, CO₂, airflow, and root zone, steered by stage and by the numbers. The room is the plant's whole world.

III

Process

Repeatable systems for every task from clone to package. Great flower once is luck; great flower every cycle is process.

IV

The Cure

Where most flower is lost. A slow dry and a real cure finish what the room started — terpenes, smoothness, and shelf life.

V

Consistency

The same quality in room ten as in room one. Consistency is what separates a brand from a batch.

VI

For the Plant

Advance the industry, advance the plant. Share what works, raise the standard, and leave the craft better than you found it.

Great cannabis isn't grown by accident, and it isn't grown by average. For the plant. For the growers.

Case study

Four rooms, 2,420 square feet, designed to run itself.

A craft-scale license holder had a 2,420 sq ft building, a tight budget, and a plan drawn by someone who'd never harvested a room. The layout wasted a third of the canopy, the HVAC was sized for offices, and there was no dry room at all.

I redesigned it as a four-room perpetual program: propagation/veg, a mother room, a dedicated dry room, and dual flower rooms on a staggered cadence — with mechanical sizing built from the actual transpiration load, a lighting plan by stage, benching chosen for labor, zoned fertigation, and room controllers feeding a single dashboard.
